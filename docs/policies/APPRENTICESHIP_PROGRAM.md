# The EFD Bench Apprenticeship

Status: **PROPOSED — v0.1, 2026-09-30.** Written at the owner's request ("go ahead and just make decisions
as you see fit"). Every number and rule here is a decision the owner can veto; nothing is built yet except
what "Already live" lists. The research behind it is in [`APPRENTICESHIP.md`](APPRENTICESHIP.md).

**What this program is:** the path from "can't hold a job" to **bench jeweler** — repair and fabrication
at the bench. Design and custom work is a separate, later curriculum for jewelers moving up.

---

| Level | Pay | What you do | You move up when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Apprentice I** — Finishing | $15 / clocked hr | Clean, polish, rhodium, prep and finish other jewelers' work | Safety signed off · 120 hours · finishing reps met · Stage I check passed |

| **Apprentice III** — Bench work | $19 / clocked hr | Gold sizing, retipping, repronging, small stone setting, laser welding | 600 hours total · Stage III reps met · **the bench test** passed |

**The rules that hold at every apprentice level** (already live, PR #140): paid by the clock as a shop
expense; never holds a job; the jeweler who holds the job is credited it in full.

**Expect** roughly 6–12 months part-time to reach the bench test. A full-time apprentice could do it faster —
the reps and the streaks, not the calendar, decide.

## How progress is measured

### A rep counts only when the job passes QC

The apprentice logs what they did on a ticket (see "How it works inside EFD"). **That rep only counts once
the ticket passes QC.** A tap on a job that comes back from QC doesn't count, and it breaks the streak.

- The mentor never enters anything. QC already happens on every job.
- This follows the UK apprenticeship standard, which does not accept self-assessment as evidence.

### Every skill has a minimum and a streak

A skill is **done** when both are true:

- **Minimum** — at least this many reps logged.
- **Streak** — the most recent reps in a row all passed QC with no rework.

The streak is what shows competence. The minimum stops a lucky run from counting. A failed QC resets that
skill's streak to zero; it never takes away reps already earned.

### Practice counts toward the minimum, not the streak

The **first reps of any new skill are done on shop practice stock**, not customer pieces (see "Risk"
below). Practice reps count toward the minimum. The streak has to be earned on customer work, because
that's the work that has to be right.

### Hours are a floor, not the test

Each level has a minimum number of clocked hours before moving up. Hours come straight from the time
clock. They stop someone rushing a level; they don't prove anything on their own.

### Moving up is automatic — and never goes backward

When every requirement for a level is met, **the raise happens.** No negotiation. That's how registered
apprenticeships work, and it's fair to the apprentice. Pay never goes down: if skills slip, the streak
resets and progress pauses, but the rate stays.

## Apprentice I — Finishing ($15)

**What you do:** the finishing half of other jewelers' jobs. Clean, pre-polish and polish; rhodium plate
white gold; ultrasonic and steam; clean up after sizing; prepare pieces for pickup.

**Before touching a torch, laser or chemical:** a safety walkthrough, signed off by the mentor:

- Torch: lighting, flame types, shutting down, gas safety.
- Laser: eye protection, never bypassing the interlock.
- Chemicals: pickle, rhodium and plating solutions, ultrasonic solution — handling, storage, disposal.
- Rotary tools and the polishing motor: guards, loose hair and sleeves, catching a chain.
- Where the extinguisher, first-aid kit and eyewash are.

**Skills to move up:**

| Skill | Catalog task | Minimum | Streak |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Clean and polish | Clean and Polish | 60 | last 15 pass QC |
| Rhodium plate | *Rhodium Plate — needs adding to the catalog* | 25 | last 10 pass QC |

**Stage I check** (the Apprentice line already on the ladder): polish and rhodium a customer piece to shop
standard without wearing detail — edges crisp, hallmarks readable, stone tables flat.

**Hours to move up:** 120 clocked.

## Apprentice II — Bench basics ($17)

**What you do:** the whole task on simple jobs, on the mentor's tickets, with the mentor checking. Still
hourly, still the mentor's job.

| Skill | Catalog tasks | Minimum | Streak |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sterling sizing (up and down) | Size Down · Size up | 40 | last 10 pass QC |
| Jump rings | Add 18gu Jump Ring (both) | 30 | last 10 |
| Soldering rings together | Solder Together per Ring | 15 | last 5 |
| Chain repair (solder) | Break in chain | 20 | last 8 |
| Clasp repair | Clasp Repair | 15 | last 5 |
| Check and tighten | Check & Tighten (both) | 20 | last 8 |

**Stage II check:** solder a jump ring closed with no visible seam; size a sterling ring down one size —
straight, round, no pits or porosity (not timed).

**Hours to move up:** 300 clocked, total.

## Apprentice III — Bench work ($19)

**What you do:** the harder repairs, still on the mentor's tickets.

| Skill | Catalog tasks | Minimum | Streak |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gold sizing (up and down) | Size Down · Size up (gold) | 60 — at least 15 size-ups | last 10 |
| Retipping | Retip prongs | 40 prongs | last 12 prongs |
| Repronging | Reprong | 10 | last 5 |
| Setting small stones | Set Stone less than 0.49ct | 30 | last 10 |
| Laser welding | Laser Weld | 30 | last 10 |
| Assessing a walk-in | *(intake shadowing — see below)* | 20 tickets | last 10 need no correction |

That brings sizing to **100 rings** total and stone setting to **30** — the owner's original numbers.

**Assessing a walk-in:** the apprentice looks at an incoming piece and writes down which catalog tasks it
needs; the jeweler taking it in compares. It counts when the jeweler didn't have to change anything that
affects the price.

**Hours to sit the bench test:** 600 clocked, total.

## The bench test

The step from Apprentice III to bench jeweler — and from hourly pay to per-task pay. Built on Jewelers of
America's beginner exam (8 jobs, 19 hours, graded by checklist), cut to EFD's catalog.

### Stations

One or two sittings, on **shop stock, not customer pieces**, graded by the owner under a 10× loupe.

| # | Station | Time allowed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Size a 14k yellow ring **down** one size | 1.25 × catalog time |
| 2 | Size a 14k white ring **up** one size, adding stock | 1.25 × catalog time |
| 3 | Retip four prongs on a head to match each other | 1.25 × catalog time |
| 4 | Set a round stone in a four-prong head, then check and tighten the rest | 1.25 × catalog time |
| 5 | Chain: solder one break, laser one break, replace a clasp | 1.25 × catalog time |
| 6 | Laser: close a seam and fill porosity without undercutting | 1.25 × catalog time |
| 7 | Finish: polish and rhodium a white-gold piece to delivery standard | 1.25 × catalog time |
| 8 | Intake: five pieces from a practice box — choose the tasks and price them | 30 minutes |

### How it's graded

- **Each station has a written checklist** of pass/fail items — fabrication, soldering, setting,
  finishing, delivery — the way JA grades. Example items: seam invisible at 10×; ring round on the
  mandrel; stone within 5° of level; prong contact 33–50% of the stone; no excess solder visible to the
  eye; no tool marks; no dirt, grease or water spots; ready to hand a customer.
- **A station passes at 75%** of its items acceptable.
- **Some items fail the station on their own**, whatever the score: a damaged or chipped stone, a loose
  stone, a visible sizing seam, pitted or burned metal, a sizing more than a quarter size off, a task
  choice on the intake station that changes the price by more than 20%.
- **Three attempts per station.** A failed station is retaken alone, two weeks later. Stations already
  passed stay passed.

### Why the time limit is 1.25×

As a bench jeweler you're paid the catalog time, however long the job really takes. At 1.25× catalog time,
$24 per catalog hour works out to **$19.20 per hour actually worked** — no less than the $19 an Apprentice
III makes. So passing can't cut your pay.

### Or: Jewelers of America's certification

An apprentice may instead pass **JA's Certified Beginner Bench Jeweler** exam. EFD accepts it in place of
its own test. It's external, customers recognize it, and the apprentice keeps the credential. **EFD pays
the fee if they pass** (about $1,600 in 2024 — $300 registration plus test materials priced off gold). It's
a real retention benefit, and it costs less than losing someone after a year of training.

## Bench jeweler — what we expect ($24 per catalog hour)

You now claim and hold your own repairs, and you're paid the catalog time for each task when it passes QC.

**The bar** (the Bench jeweler line already on the ladder, kept):

- Size 14k up and down within catalog time with an invisible seam and matching finish.
- Retip four prongs on a customer-grade head with even, symmetrical tips.
- Tighten and replace a stone in a prong setting; check and secure all the rest.
- Repair a chain (solder and laser) and replace a clasp.
- Laser weld: seam and fill without undercutting.
- Assess a walk-in and pick the right catalog tasks with no admin correction.

**Ongoing expectations:**

- **First-pass QC of 90% or better** over your last 30 jobs. Below that, we talk — it's a coaching trigger,
  not a pay cut.
- Your work still goes through QC by someone else. Self-QC comes with senior.
- Take the jobs that come in, not just the ones that pay best per minute.
- Customer pieces in your custody are your responsibility (artisan terms §8).

## After the bench

The **senior** and **master** rungs are the owner's existing ladder requirements, with two changes:

- **Master drops from $50 to $40** — the top rung (owner, 2026-09-30). $50 is the owner's own rate.
- **Senior adds a number:** first-pass QC of 95% or better over the last 50 jobs, alongside the existing
  requirements (platinum sizing, head replacement and re-setting, channel and bead-set repair, hollow
  chain, and a clean week of QCing other people's work).

Design and custom work is its own curriculum, for jewelers moving up — not part of this one.

## Things the program has to handle (that nobody has asked about yet)

### 1. Apprentices are probably employees, not contractors — check before scaling this

*Not legal advice — take this to an accountant or employment lawyer.* EFD pays everyone as a 1099
contractor through Stripe Connect. An apprentice paid by the hour, trained and directed by the shop, on the
shop's premises, with the shop's tools, is the textbook case the IRS and the Department of Labor treat as an
**employee**. Misclassification can mean back payroll taxes and penalties, and there's no workers'
compensation for someone using a torch and a laser. The likely answer is to run apprentices through a
payroll service as W-2 employees, and keep bench jewelers (who choose their jobs and are paid per task)
as contractors.

### 2. Who pays when an apprentice damages a customer's piece

The shop does. Never the apprentice — in most places, docking wages for damage is illegal, and it would
teach people to hide mistakes. The jeweler holding the job is responsible for it (artisan terms §8), and an
apprentice works under that jeweler. The real protection is the practice rule: **new skills are learned on
shop stock first.**

### 3. Practice stock costs money

Sterling sizing stock, scrap shanks, practice heads and cheap stones. Budget it as a training cost. The
logbook records practice reps separately, so the owner can see what it costs.

### 4. It's worth registering

The Department of Labor's national model for bench-jeweler apprenticeships is **MJSA's Mentor &
Apprenticeship Program** — a free 50-week curriculum for MJSA members, 200+ videos, and certification. A
program registered with the Department of Labor can qualify for state and federal grants, tax credits and
tuition support. EFD's program could adopt MJSA's curriculum as its lesson plan and use this document for
the levels, pay and tracking.

### 5. Reviews on a schedule

A sit-down at **3, 6 and 12 months**: what's done, what's stuck. If an apprentice hasn't moved up a level
in 6 months, it's a conversation about whether this is working — for both sides.

### 6. A one-page written agreement

Rates, levels, what moves you up, the review schedule, the damage rule, and that training has no clawback
if someone leaves. The artisan terms don't cover apprentices; they describe a contractor relationship.

### 7. Current apprentices

- **Michelle** says she's already partway through a bench apprenticeship. She sits the Stage I check now;
  if she passes, she starts at Apprentice II ($17) with her Stage I reps waived.
- **Henlie** starts at Apprentice I ($15). She can't sign in yet (no password) — she needs to use
  **Forgot password** on admin.engelfinedesign.com before she can clock in or log anything.

### 8. Catalog gaps this needs filled

- **Rhodium Plate** isn't a task. It needs to be one to be counted — and it's worth checking whether it's
  being charged for at all today.
- "Assessing a walk-in" isn't a task either; it's logged as intake shadowing (see "How it works").

## How it works inside EFD — implementation plan

### What already exists (PR #140)

- The **Apprentice rung** on the pay ladder is the apprentice flag (`employment.payTier === 'apprentice'`,
  `src/services/pay/apprenticeRules.js`).
- **The time clock** only works for apprentices; shifts become payable labor logs.
- **Apprentices can't hold a job** — kept out of every assignable-jeweler list, refused at claim and
  hand-off, and any bench credit that lands on one is held in Labor Review.

### Design rules

- **Nothing is hard-coded.** Rep counts, streaks, hours and rates live in Settings, editable by the owner.
- **The logbook never pays anyone.** It records work; pay stays with the clock and the jeweler's labor
  logs.
- **The QC pass is the only thing that turns a logged rep into a counted one.**
- **Raises go through the existing, privileged pay-rate route** (`PATCH /api/users/[userID]/pay-rate`),
  never around it.

### Phase 1 — Levels on the ladder (small)

- Replace the single `apprentice` rung with **`apprentice-1`, `apprentice-2`, `apprentice-3`** at $15 /
  $17 / $19, and move bench jeweler to $24, senior to $32, master to $40 (`DEFAULT_LADDER` in
  `src/services/pay/payLadder.js`; editable under Settings → Pay ladder).
- **`isApprentice` becomes "payTier starts with `apprentice`"** — so all three levels keep every rule from
  PR #140 (clock, never holds a job, the Labor Review backstop).
- The artisan page shows the level and what the next one needs.
- Move Henlie to `apprentice-1`; Michelle to `apprentice-1` pending her Stage I check.

### Phase 2 — The curriculum as settings (small)

`adminSettings.apprenticeship` holds the curriculum as data:

```
levels: [
  { key: 'apprentice-1', label: 'Finishing', hoursToAdvance: 120,
    signoffs: ['safety', 'stage-1-check'],
    skills: [
      { key: 'polish',  label: 'Clean and polish', taskIDs: [...], minimum: 60, streak: 15 },
      { key: 'rhodium', label: 'Rhodium plate',    taskIDs: [...], minimum: 25, streak: 10 },
    ] },
  ...
]
```

A Settings screen edits it. Validated on save the way the pay ladder is (`normalizeLadder`).

### Phase 3 — The logbook (the main build)

**New collection `apprenticeContributions`:**

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `contributionID` | id |
| `apprenticeUserID` | who did it |
| `repairID` / `workOrderID` | the job — or null for practice |
| `taskID`, `skillKey` | which catalog task, which curriculum skill |
| `quantity` | e.g. prongs retipped |
| `kind` | `job` or `practice` |
| `status` | `pending` → `counted` or `failed` |
| `holderUserID` | the jeweler holding the job at the time |
| `createdAt`, `resolvedAt` | when logged, when QC decided |

**The apprentice's page** (`/dashboard/apprentice`): the same scanner pattern as My Bench
(`ContinuousBarcodeScanner` + `services/bench/scanActions.js`). Scan a ticket → the ticket's own catalog
tasks appear as buttons, plus Polish and Rhodium → tap what you did (with a quantity for prongs) → saved as
`pending`. A "Practice" button logs a rep with no ticket. Only apprentices can log, and only on an open
ticket.

**The QC hook:** when a repair passes QC — the same place its labor logs are credited — every `pending`
contribution on it becomes `counted`. When QC sends it back to the bench, they become `failed`. One place,
so nothing else can mark a rep counted.

### Phase 4 — Progress (small)

A pure function, `progressFor(curriculum, contributions, clockedHours, signoffs)`, returns for each skill
its count, current streak and whether it's done, and for the level whether it's ready. Two screens read it:

- **The apprentice's own progress** — what's done, what's next, hours so far.
- **The owner's view per apprentice** — what they've done, what they've learned, how many hours. Exactly
  the data the owner asked to be able to see.

### Phase 5 — Sign-offs and the bench test (medium)

- **`apprenticeSignoffs`** — the safety walkthrough and each stage check: who signed off, when, notes.
- **`benchTests`** — per station: the checklist with each item marked acceptable or unacceptable, which
  items were auto-fail, attempt number, time taken against the allowed time, pass or fail. The checklists
  themselves live in settings.

### Phase 6 — Moving up (small)

When a level is complete, the owner gets a notification: *"Henlie is ready for Apprentice II."* **Approve**
calls the pay-rate route with the next rung. Passing the bench test moves someone to `bench-jeweler` —
which ends the apprentice rules, so they can claim jobs, and the clock stops being available to them.

### Order and rough size

| Phase | Size | Can start |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Levels on the ladder | hours | now |
| 2 Curriculum as settings | a day | after 1 |
| 3 Logbook + QC hook | 1–2 days | after 2 |
| 4 Progress screens | a day | after 3 |
| 5 Sign-offs + bench test record | 1–2 days | after 2 |
| 6 Moving up | half a day | after 4 and 5 |

Phase 1 is independent and useful on its own: it's the raise schedule.
